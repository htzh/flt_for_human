# `ModularCurve/ModularPolynomialIrreducible.lean` — notation dictionary

The module proves that the level-`ℓ` datum is irreducible over `ℚ(j)` (`phiIrreducible_of_splits`)
and symmetric (`evalSymm_of_splits`, `ModularPolynomialData.evalSymm_of_irreducible`), given that its
coefficients split into the conjugate product. Almost all of its notation exists to keep the **three
images of one formal polynomial** apart, and the part hardest to read is the family of ring homs that
move between them — so those come first, alone.

Module: [ModularPolynomialIrreducible.lean](../../FLTForHuman/ModularCurve/ModularPolynomialIrreducible.lean)

## The maps

All of them are `→+*`. Three things distinguish them, and reading them side by side is the point:
which slot they act on (`X` of `Φ`, the nome `q`, or the outer variable `Y`), which coefficient ring
they land in, and whether they change the level.

| map | source → target | action | where it is read |
|---|---|---|---|
| `evalAtJ` | `ℤ[X] → ℚ((q))` | `X ↦ j(q)` | `data.eval_eq_zero` |
| `evalAtJqN N` | `ℤ[X] → ℚ((q))` | `X ↦ j(q ^ N)` | the `Y`-slot of `data.eval_eq_zero` |
| `evalAtJAdj` | `ℤ[X] → ℚ[j(q)]` | `X ↦ j(q)` | `Φ₀` |
| `evalAtJGen` | `ℤ[X] → ℚ(j(q))` | `X ↦ j(q)` | `data.toAdjoin` |
| `algebraMap ℚ[j(q)] ℚ(j(q))` | `ℚ[j(q)] ↪ ℚ(j(q))` | inclusion | the Gauss step `phiIrreducible_iff_irreducible_map_evalAtJAdj` |
| `coeffEmb K` | `ℚ((q)) → K((q))` | coefficients `ℚ → K`, `q` fixed | every statement at the `K((q))` level |
| `qExpand K N` | `K((q)) → K((q))` | `q ↦ q ^ N`: the coefficient at `m` moves to `N * m` | `jqN N`, `conj 0`, inside `qEmbedT` |
| `qEmbedT K ℓ` (**private**) | `ℚ((q)) → K((q))` | `q ↦ q ^ ℓ` then `ℚ → K`, so `j(q) ↦ j(q ^ ℓ)` | the `X`-slot spelling of `Φ₁` |
| `adjoinEmbedT K ℓ` (**private**) | `ℚ[j(q)] → K((q))` | the same substitution, but on the coefficient ring | `Φ₁ = Φ₀.map σ`, `A ^ σ`, the root count, `hnoRoot` |
| `qTwist u` | `K((q)) → K((q))` | `q ↦ u * q`: the coefficient at `m` is multiplied by `u ^ m` | the conjugates `conj b.succ`, and the twists of §11 |

Two identities collapse the spellings, so the picture is smaller than the table:

- `coeffEmb_qExpand` — `(coeffEmb K).comp (qExpand ℚ N) = (qExpand K N).comp (coeffMap (algebraMap ℚ K))`.
  The right-hand side is by definition `qEmbedT K N`, so the pin's composite
  `(coeffEmb K) ∘ (qExpand ℚ ℓ)` **is** `qEmbedT K ℓ`, not a second map.
- `adjoinEmbedT_comp_evalAtJAdj` — `(adjoinEmbedT K ℓ).comp evalAtJAdj = (qEmbedT K ℓ).comp evalAtJ`:
  the two rows below agree.

```
row 1   ℤ[X] --evalAtJAdj--> ℚ[j(q)] --adjoinEmbedT K ℓ--> K((q))
row 2   ℤ[X] --evalAtJ-----> ℚ((q))  --qEmbedT K ℓ-------> K((q))
```

and the fraction field sits above row 1: `evalAtJGen` is `evalAtJAdj` followed by
`algebraMap ℚ[j(q)] ℚ(j(q))` (`algebraMap_comp_evalAtJAdj`), which is what makes the Gauss step a
statement about the same polynomial at two coefficient rings.

## 2. The datum

`data : ModularPolynomialData ℓ` is `Φ ∈ ℤ[X][Y]`, monic of degree `ψ(ℓ) = ℓ + 1`, vanishing at
`(j(q), j(q ^ ℓ))`. It is the level-`ℓ` modular polynomial with the three facts the cone needs, and
it is unique at a prime (`ModularPolynomialData.eq_of_prime`), so "the datum at `ℓ`" is a definite
object. Read `data.Φ` as that polynomial; the other fields are its certificate.

What the datum does **not** carry is the splitting of the fibre at `X = j(q ^ ℓ)`. That is a separate
hypothesis (`hsplit`) of the theorems that need it, proved elsewhere from the descended coefficient
family; it is not a consequence of the three facts.

## 3. Levels — the objects

| name | definition | coefficient ring | `Y` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Φ` | `data.Φ` | `ℤ` | formal |
| `Φ₀` | `data.Φ.map evalAtJAdj`, i.e. `Φ(j(q), Y)` | `ℚ[j(q)]` | formal |
| `Φ₁` | `Φ₀.map (adjoinEmbedT K ℓ)`, i.e. `Φ(j(q ^ ℓ), Y)` | `K((q))` | formal |
| `data.toAdjoin` | `data.Φ.map evalAtJGen`, i.e. `Φ(j(q), Y)` | `ℚ(j(q))` | formal |
| `A`, `B` | the two factors in a factorisation of `Φ₀` | `ℚ[j(q)]` | formal |
| `A ^ σ`, `B ^ σ` | informal for `A.map (adjoinEmbedT K ℓ)` and the same for `B` | `K((q))` | formal |

`Φ₀` and `Φ₁` are introduced by `set` at the top of `phiIrreducible_of_splits`. Irreducibility is
decided at `Φ₀`: `PhiIrreducible data` is `Irreducible data.toAdjoin`, and Gauss
(`phiIrreducible_iff_irreducible_map_evalAtJAdj`) moves it to `Φ₀` across `ℚ[j(q)] ⊂ ℚ(j(q))`.

## 4. Points — the `Y`-variable

| point | in | role |
|---|---|---|
| `coeffEmb K jq` | `K((q))` | `j(q)`, which is `conj ℓ ζ (0 : Fin ℓ).succ`; the point at which the conjugate product vanishes, hence the factor the root count kills |
| `conj ℓ ζ i` | `K((q))` | the `ℓ + 1` roots of `Φ₁`; the `ℓ` finite ones are all hit from one, by twist invariance of the coefficients |

## 5. Three spellings of one polynomial

The level-`ℓ` image `Φ₁` is written three ways, and recognising them is most of the reading:

- `Φ₀.map (adjoinEmbedT K ℓ)` — the level change on the coefficient ring;
- `data.Φ.map ((qEmbedT K ℓ).comp evalAtJ)` — the same map, `X` slot first;
- `data.Φ.map (((coeffEmb K).comp (qExpand ℚ ℓ)).comp evalAtJ)` — the pin's spelling, used in the
  hypothesis `hsplit`; `coeffEmb_qExpand` says it is the same composite.

## 6. The centre of the proof: `hnoRoot`

Everything else in `phiIrreducible_of_splits` is degree bookkeeping; the content is
`hnoRoot : ∀ β : adjoinJq, Φ₀.eval β ≠ 0`, that `Φ(j(q), Y)` has no root in `ℚ[j(q)]`.

Step 1 — the level change, from a root in `ℚ[j(q)]` to a root in `K((q))`. This is a congruence,
not a computation: a coefficient ring hom takes a root to a root, which is mathlib's
`Polynomial.IsRoot.map` (`(Φ₀.map σ).eval (σ β) = σ (Φ₀.eval β)`), so with `σ = adjoinEmbedT K ℓ`
and `Φ₁ = Φ₀.map σ` the vanishing at `β` gives one at `σ β`:

```lean
    rw [← hsplit₁, hΦ₁]
    exact Polynomial.IsRoot.map hβ
```

Hence `σ β` is a root of the conjugate product, and a product of linear factors vanishes only if a
factor does — `exists_eq_conj_of_eval_phiProd_eq_zero`, the one root-counting step of the proof:
`∃ i, σ β = conj ℓ ζ i`.

Step 2 — the two cases of `Fin (ℓ + 1)`:

- **the extra conjugate** (`i = 0`, so `σ β = conj 0 = j(q ^ (ℓ * ℓ))`). This is where the
  arithmetic input enters, and it is one named descent:
  `eq_jqN_of_adjoinEmbedT_eq_conj_zero` — a `calc` through `qEmbedT = adjoinEmbedT = conj 0 =
  qEmbedT (jqN ℓ)`, so injectivity of `qEmbedT` gives `β = j(q ^ ℓ)`. Then `j(q ^ ℓ) ∈ ℚ[j(q)]`,
  which `jqN_not_mem_adjoin_jq` rules out — the positivity of `j`'s coefficients at `n = ℓ` inside
  a twist-sum coefficient. This case is where the module's one arithmetic input, `one_le_coeff_jq`,
  is consumed; the argument is §11.
- **a finite conjugate** (`i = b.succ`, so `σ β = j(ζ ^ b * q)`). No non-membership is needed; the
  two sides are separated by their `q⁻¹` coefficient:
  - `j(q) = q⁻¹ + O(1)`, and the nome twist `q ↦ ζ ^ b q` multiplies the `q⁻¹` coefficient by
    `ζ ^ (-b)`, so *every* finite conjugate has coefficient `(ζ ^ b)⁻¹ ≠ 0` at `-1`
    (`conj_succ_coeff_neg_one`);
  - the level-`ℓ` substitution moves the pole from `q⁻¹` to `q ^ (-ℓ)`, so a level-`ℓ` image is a
    series in `q ^ ℓ` and has coefficient `0` at `-1` (`ℓ ∤ -1`, `coeff_neg_one_adjoinEmbedT`).

  `-1` is the slot where the pole of `j` of order `1` sits: it is nonzero after every twist and
  empty after `q ↦ q ^ ℓ`, so it separates the two kinds of conjugate.

Why the two branches are different in kind: the two nome operations act on different things in a
`q`-expansion. `qExpand` *moves the exponents* (`m ↦ ℓ * m`), so the support lands in `ℓ ℤ` and the
pole `q ^ (-1)` of `j` becomes `q ^ (-ℓ)`; `qTwist u` *rescales the coefficients* (exponent fixed,
the coefficient at `m` multiplied by `u ^ m`), leaving the support — and with it the simple pole at
`-1` — in place. The relation `ζ ^ (b * ℓ) = 1` ties them together: it makes the level map invisible
to the twists (`qTwist_comp_qEmbedT`), which is what lets the root count (§7) spread one vanishing
over all `ℓ` finite conjugates, and it is also why twisting leaves the `q ^ ℓ` coefficient alone,
which is the twist sum of §11.

`-1` is not the only exponent that would separate the two sides — any `m` with `ℓ ∤ m` is empty on a
level-`ℓ` image, and `+1` would do as well — but it is the only one where the conjugate's side costs
nothing: `j`'s coefficients vanish below `-1` and are `1` at `-1` itself (the pole), so a twist leaves
that slot nonzero without any arithmetic. At `+1` one would need `jq.coeff 1 ≥ 1`; the module spends
its one arithmetic input instead at `n = ℓ` in the twist sum of §11, where the exponent has to be a
*multiple* of `ℓ` for the `ℓ` twists to add up (`∑_b ζ ^ (b * m) = ℓ` iff `ℓ ∣ m`).

## 7. The root count: `le_natDegree_of_eval_map_jqK_eq_zero`

"If `D ≠ 0` over `ℚ[j(q)]` and its level-`ℓ` image vanishes at `j(q)`, then `ℓ ≤ D.natDegree`." Read
off three named facts:

| name | statement |
|---|---|
| `hconj` | the `ℓ` finite conjugates `b ↦ conj b.succ` are distinct (`conj_injective` + `Fin.succ_injective`) |
| `hroots` | the level-`ℓ` image vanishes at every one of them (`eval_map_conj_succ_eq_zero`: `ζ^b` fixes `ℚ[j(q)]`'s image, since the image is a series in `q ^ ℓ`) |
| `hDσ` | the image is nonzero (`adjoinEmbedT_injective`) |

then `deg D ^ σ ≥ ℓ` by the root bound, and `deg D ^ σ ≤ deg D` (`natDegree_map_le`) gives the
claim; the closing `calc` is exactly these two inequalities.

| `hsum` | `A.natDegree + B.natDegree = ℓ + 1` |
| `hAdvd`, `hBdvd` | `A ∣ Φ₀`, `B ∣ Φ₀` |

| `hB1`, `hA1` | the surviving factor has degree `≤ 1`, so it is a unit by `isUnit_of_dvd_of_natDegree_le_one` |

The one generic step of the proof is `isUnit_of_dvd_of_natDegree_le_one`: a divisor of degree `≤ 1`
with unit leading coefficient of a polynomial with no root is a unit. It mentions no datum and no
level; its two cases are degree `0` (unit constant) and degree `1` (would have the root
`-u⁻¹ * p.coeff 0`).

## 10. The same fact elsewhere in the port

`j(q ^ p) ∉ ℚ(j(q))` is developed more than once, at different levels, and the developments are
*nested*, not parallel:

| statement | module | proof |
|---|---|---|
| `jqN_not_mem_adjoin_jq` (private, §6): `jqN p ∉ ℚ[j(q)]` | here | the `q ^ p` coefficient of the twist sum, against the positivity of `j`'s coefficients at `n = p` (§11) |
| `jqN_prime_not_mem_base` (private): `jqN r ∉ ℚ⟮jq⟯` | `FunctionFieldGeneration/Nonmembership.lean` | `finrank_adjoin_jqN_eq_of_prime r`: the relative degree is `r + 1 > 1`, so no field element is `j(q ^ r)` |
| `prime_notMem_levelField`: `levelSeries jg p ∉ levelField jg M` for `p ∤ M`, over a general coefficient field | `FunctionFieldGeneration/Generic/PrimeNonmembership.lean` | that degree fact as the base case (`hbase`), with the slot product and the `Φ_p` dichotomy for the level step |

The first and second are the polynomial-ring and the field-level forms of one statement — the field
one is the stronger conclusion — and the order between them is forced by the imports:
`Nonmembership.lean` imports `ModularPolynomialProperties`, hence this module, and the finrank
theorem it spends comes from `exists_phiIrreducible_evalSymm`, i.e. from `phiIrreducible_of_splits`
and so from `hnoRoot`. The coefficient-level refutation proved here is therefore the input of the
degree-level one; it cannot be replaced by it, and deleting it would break the chain.

## 11. The twist-sum refutation (the non-membership)

`jqN_not_mem_adjoin_jq` says `j(q ^ ℓ)` is not a polynomial in `j(q)`. Suppose `P(j(q)) = j(q ^ ℓ)`
with `P ∈ ℚ[X]`. The proof is **two lemmas and one line combining them**, and they are worth keeping
apart because they are about different objects: one is algebra of the conjugate product, the other is
a statement about `P`.

### A. The conjugate product is not rational at degree `ℓ - 1`

`sum_conj_succ_ne_ratC` — for every `c : ℚ`, `∑_b j(ζ ^ b q) ≠ ratC K c`. Two facts:

- the sum is twist-invariant: a twist permutes the conjugates, so the sum is fixed by all of them,
  hence a series in `q ^ ℓ`;
- its coefficient at `ℓ` is `ℓ * jq.coeff ℓ ≠ 0`: each conjugate has the same coefficient there
  (`ζ ^ (b * ℓ) = 1`), and positivity gives `jq.coeff ℓ ≥ 1` (`coeff_sum_conj_succ_self`,
  `coeff_sum_conj_succ_ne_zero`, the single use of `one_le_coeff_jq`).

A rational constant vanishes at every positive exponent (`coeff_ratC_of_ne`), so it cannot be this
series.

Why degree `ℓ - 1` in particular: the coefficients of `∏_b (Y - j(ζ ^ b q))` at `Y ^ k` are the
elementary symmetric functions of the conjugates, all twist-invariant; the top one (`k = ℓ`) is `1`,
which *is* rational, and the next one (`k = ℓ - 1`) is the sum itself — linear in the conjugates, so
its `q ^ ℓ` coefficient is just `ℓ` times one known coefficient. That is why the proof reads one step
below the leading coefficient.

### B. Vieta puts `P` there

`map_ratC_eq_prod_add_of_aeval_jq_eq` — over `K`, `P(j(q)) = j(q ^ ℓ)` forces the *identity of
polynomials*

```
P(Y) = ∏_b (Y - j(ζ ^ b q)) + j(q ^ ℓ)
```

whose coefficient form is `sum_conj_succ_eq_ratC_of_aeval_jq_eq`:
`∑_b j(ζ ^ b q) = ratC K (-(P.coeff (ℓ - 1)))`. Three inputs:

1. **`P` is monic of degree `ℓ`** — `j(q) = q⁻¹ + O(1)` makes `P(j(q))` have pole order `deg P` with
   leading coefficient `lc P`, while `j(q ^ ℓ) = q ^ (-ℓ) + O(1)`
   (`natDegree_eq_of_aeval_jq_eq_jqN`, `monic_of_aeval_jq_eq_jqN`);
2. **base change** to the cyclotomic field, coefficients along `ratC`
   (`eval₂_ratC_jqK_of_aeval_jq_eq`);
3. **the twists are roots**: `τ_b` fixes `j(q ^ ℓ)` (since `ζ ^ (b * ℓ) = 1`) and sends `j(q)` to
   `j(ζ ^ b q)` (`eval₂_ratC_conj_succ_of_aeval_jq_eq`), so the `ℓ` distinct conjugates are all roots
   of `P(Y) = j(q ^ ℓ)`; two monic degree-`ℓ` polynomials agreeing at `ℓ` points differ by a
   constant, which the common value identifies.

### Combination

`P.coeff (ℓ - 1)` is rational (it is a coefficient of `P ∈ ℚ[X]`), and A says the sum is never
rational, while B says they are equal:

```lean
  exact sum_conj_succ_ne_ratC ℓ ζ hζ _ (sum_conj_succ_eq_ratC_of_aeval_jq_eq ℓ ζ hζ hP)
```

Neither `jqN_not_mem_adjoin_jq` nor `sum_conj_succ_ne_ratC` takes a positivity hypothesis:
`one_le_coeff_jq` is applied *inside*, at `n = ℓ`, so the module's one arithmetic input appears
exactly once, as an internal fact, and the non-membership is unconditional.

### What the value `j(q ^ ℓ)` contributes

Split the argument by what it uses from the right-hand side:

- A uses only that the value is *fixed by all `ℓ` twists*, i.e. lies in the image of `qExpand ℓ`
  (support in `ℓ ℤ`);
- in B, the pole order of the value (`j(q ^ ℓ) = q ^ (-ℓ) + O(1)`, `coeff_jqN_self`,
  `coeff_jqN_of_lt`) is what makes `P` monic of degree `ℓ`; the Vieta step itself looks at no other
  feature of it — the constant it contributes vanishes at `Y ^ (ℓ - 1)`.

So the statement the argument really proves is: *no rational polynomial in `j(q)` takes a value that
is fixed by all `ℓ` twists **and** is `q ^ (-ℓ) + O(1)`*. Both halves are needed: twist-invariance
alone gives `ℓ` roots of `P(Y) = A` with no grip on `deg P` — a constant `A` is fixed by every twist
and is a polynomial in `j(q)` (take `P = A`) — while the pole order forces `deg P = ℓ` and `lc P = 1`
so that the `ℓ` roots fill the degree. `j(q ^ ℓ)` is the instance that arises, because the descent of
§6 identifies the root `β` with exactly that series; the port's lemmas are stated for it because the
pin states them that way.
