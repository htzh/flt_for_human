Modularity, computed

A workbench for the arithmetic input to Wiles's modularity theorem: give it a Weierstrass equation, and it finds the minimal model, the discriminant, the conductor, the traces of Frobenius ap by counting points, and the weight-2 eigenform q-expansion the theorem attaches to them.

Companion to math note 007 “Modularity and computation” · all notes & apps · the Lean proof: anthropics/fermats-last-theorem

Input A Weierstrass equation

Coefficients of y2 + a1xy + a3y = x3 + a2x2 + a4x + a6, as integers (the curve must be smooth, i.e. Δ ≠ 0).

What is assumed. We take the full modularity theorem as a black box (every semistable elliptic curve over ℚ is modular) and therefore do not verify semistability here. The app computes exactly the data the theorem consumes, and shows the eigenform recursion it produces. Nothing is proved by the app; the proof lives in the Lean files linked at the bottom.

Why What the computation has to do with Fermat

A counterexample ap + bp = cp (p ≥ 5 prime, abc ≠ 0, primitive) produces the Frey curve EP : y² + xy = x³ + ((bp−1−ap)/4)x² − (apbp/16)x. Its discriminant is Δ = (abc)2p/2⁸, so its bad primes are exactly the primes of abc, and it is semistable. Wiles's theorem makes it modular; Ribet's level-lowering theorem strips the bad primes one at a time, leaving a nonzero weight-2 cusp form of level 2; and S₂(Γ₀(2)) = 0. Contradiction.

The app is a window on the first and last of those links: it computes the level N that Wiles's theorem feeds on, and the coefficient sequence that Ribet's congruence then has to force to zero at level 2. (A genuine Frey curve requires a genuine counterexample, which does not exist; the “Frey-type” preset uses arbitrary a, b, p of the right shape to show the arithmetic.)

Engine: ec.js (self-contained BigInt arithmetic). Minimal models and conductors come from Tate's algorithm; the eigenform coefficients come from the Hecke recursions. The level-11 check uses the eta product η(τ)²η(11τ)² of the newform 11.2.a.a.
