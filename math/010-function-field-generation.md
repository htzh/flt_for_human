# Function field generation for `X₀(N)`

This is the tenth `math/` note. Note [009](009-hecke-jacobian-commute.md) built the
Jacobian $`J_0(N)`$ out of $`q`$-expansions and used, as one of its inputs, the
classical description of the function field of $`X_0(N)`$: over $`\mathbb{Q}`$ it is
generated by the two functions $`j(q)`$ and $`j(q^N)`$. That input is the theorem of
this note,

```lean
-- Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean, line 8
theorem ModularCurve.functionFieldGeneration (N : ℕ) [NeZero N] : FunctionFieldGeneration N
```

whose content is: for every divisor $`d \mid N`$,

$$j(q^d) \\;\in\\; \mathbb{Q}\bigl(j(q),\\, j(q^N)\bigr).$$

In Diamond–Shurman the statement is an exercise, and a compact one: with $`X_0(N)`$
available as a Riemann surface and the $`j`$-line below it, generation is a statement
about function fields, and $`j`$ separates enough points. The formalization has no
Riemann existence theorem and no compact Riemann surface. Everything happens inside a
single fixed field of Laurent series, starting from the explicit $`q`$-expansion of
$`j`$, and the price of that choice is a long induction. This note makes the induction
readable. The Lean fixes the route; we tell the mathematics along it and use the Lean
only as a map.

The one genuinely analytic-looking input is the classical description of the roots of
the modular equation $`\Phi_p`$; after that everything is field theory and a strong
induction on $`N`$. We isolate that input in §3 and then follow the two halves of the
induction, *generation* (§4–§5) and *degree* (§6), to the assembly in §7.

Line-number citations point at `anthropics/fermats-last-theorem@aa2d8b3`. Where the
prose and the Lean differ, the Lean is right.

## 1. The two fields, and the geometry behind the statement

Fix the nome $`q`$ and the $`q`$-expansion

$$j(q) \\;=\\; q^{-1} + 744 + 196884\\,q + \cdots \\;\in\\; \mathbb{Q}(\\!(q)\\!).$$

The formalization stores it as an explicit Laurent series `jq`
([Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean, line 157](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean#L157)); the coefficients and
their integrality are the subject of [base/004](../base/004-the-j-invariant.md). Two
formal operations on Laurent series generate everything else:

- $`q \mapsto q^N`$: the ring endomorphism `qExpand ℚ N`, multiplying every exponent
  by $`N`$; applied to $`j(q)`$ it gives

  $$j_N \\;:=\\; j(q^N).$$

- $`q \mapsto u q`$ for a unit $`u`$ in a coefficient field $`K`$: the ring
  endomorphism `qTwist u`, which rescales the coefficient of $`q^k`$ by $`u^k`$. Its
  composite with $`q \mapsto q^e`$ is the substitution $`q \mapsto u q^e`$, and we
  abbreviate it, as the Lean does, by

  $$j(u\\,q^e) \\;:=\\; \mathrm{qExpand}_K(e)\bigl(\mathrm{qTwist}(u)\bigl(\mathrm{coeffEmb}_K\\, j(q)\bigr)\bigr).$$

  Here $`\mathrm{coeffEmb}_K`$ is the coefficient extension $`\mathbb{Q} \to K`$
  applied termwise ([Def_ModularCurve_LaurentCoeff.lean, line 81](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_LaurentCoeff.lean#L81)).
  The Lean name for $`j(uq^e)`$ is `TS K e u`. This is exactly the substitution
  $`\tau \mapsto (e\tau + \text{const})`$ written at the level of $`q`$-expansions,
  with no complex analysis involved.

The two fields of the theorem are (in Lean `modularFunctionField N` and
`modularFunctionFieldFull N`; [Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean, lines 250 and 305](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean#L250-L305)):

$$F_N \\;:=\\; \mathbb{Q}\bigl(j(q),\\, j(q^N)\bigr),$$

$$F_N^{\mathrm{full}} \\;:=\\; \mathbb{Q}\bigl(\\,j(q^d) \\;:\\; d \mid N\\,\bigr).$$

The inclusion $`F_N \subseteq F_N^{\mathrm{full}}`$ is immediate ($`j(q)`$ is
$`j(q^1)`$, and $`j(q^N)`$ has $`d = N`$). The theorem is the reverse inclusion:

$$F_N^{\mathrm{full}} \\;=\\; F_N .$$

Geometrically, $`F_N^{\mathrm{full}}`$ is the function field of the modular curve
$`X_0(N)`$ over $`\mathbb{Q}`$, presented by the degeneracy $`X_0(N) \to X(1)`$ and
all of its intermediate levels; $`F_N`$ is the subfield generated by the two
functions $`j`$ and $`j_N`$. The theorem says the intermediate levels add nothing new,
i.e. $`\mathbb{Q}(X_0(N)) = \mathbb{Q}(j, j_N)`$ — the classical Hauptmodul-style
description.

The number that governs the whole proof is the Dedekind $`\psi`$ function
([Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean, line 201](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean#L201)):

$$\psi(N) \\;:=\\; \sum_{\substack{d \mid N \\\\ d\\ \text{squarefree}}} \frac{N}{d} \\;=\\; N \prod_{p \mid N}\Bigl(1 + \frac{1}{p}\Bigr),$$

the index $`[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) : \Gamma_0(N)]`$, hence the degree of the cover
$`X_0(N) \to X(1)`$. The theorem will be proved together with the equality

$$\bigl[\\,\mathbb{Q}\bigl(j(q)\bigr)\bigl(j(q^N)\bigr) : \mathbb{Q}\bigl(j(q)\bigr)\bigr] \\;=\\; \psi(N),$$

which is the degree of the modular equation $`\Phi_N`$ and the same statement as the
irreducibility of $`\Phi_N`$ over $`\mathbb{Q}(j(q))`$. The two facts — generation and
degree — are proved by one induction, each feeding the other.

## 2. The plan: two statements and one strong induction

Because the formalization builds $`X_0(N)`$ from $`F_N^{\mathrm{full}}`$, it is
$`F_N^{\mathrm{full}}`$ that is "the curve", and $`F_N`$ that is the candidate
two-generator model. So the theorem is the collapse

$$\texttt{FunctionFieldGeneration N} \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad F_N^{\mathrm{full}} = F_N .$$

The Lean makes this precise once and for all:

```lean
-- Thm_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration_iff_full_eq.lean, line 6
theorem ModularCurve.functionFieldGeneration_iff_full_eq (N : ℕ) [NeZero N] :
    FunctionFieldGeneration N ↔ modularFunctionFieldFull N = modularFunctionField N
```

The forward direction uses $`F_N \subseteq F_N^{\mathrm{full}}`$ and the definition of
"adjoin"; the backward direction is the remark above that every
$`j(q^d)`$ is a generator of the full field. Nothing else is needed for the
equivalence ([S file, lines 9–20](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration_iff_full_eq.lean#L9-L20)).

For the induction the proof bundles the two facts at a level $`d`$:

- **(Tight)** $`\bigl[\,\mathbb{Q}(j(q))(j(q^d)) : \mathbb{Q}(j(q))\bigr] = \psi(d)`$ —
  the modular equation at level $`d`$ has the full degree;
- **(Gen)** $`F_d = F_d^{\mathrm{full}}`$ — the collapse at level $`d`$.

The induction hypothesis is the package

$$\mathrm{Hall}(N) \\;:\Longleftrightarrow\\; \forall\\, d \mid N,\\ \\ \mathrm{Tight}(d) \wedge \mathrm{Gen}(d),$$

and the engine is the single strong induction

```lean
-- S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean, lines 614–617
private theorem hall_all : ∀ N : ℕ, N ≠ 0 → Hall N := by
  intro N
  induction N using Nat.strong_induction_on with
  | _ N ih => ...
```

proved in `ModularCurve.W1.hall_all`
([lines 614–700](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L614-L700)). Once $`\mathrm{Hall}(N)`$
holds, `Gen N` is exactly `modularFunctionField N = modularFunctionFieldFull N`, and
`functionFieldGeneration_iff_full_eq` finishes.

Why does one induction carry both? Because each half at level $`d`$ needs the *other*
half only at proper divisors of $`d`$:

- **Gen $`d`$** is proved by walking down the divisor lattice of $`d`$ one prime at a
  time; each step needs $`\mathrm{Gen}(M)`$ for $`M \lt d`$, and the descent argument
  itself needs the *degree* facts at those smaller $`M`$ (§4).
- **Tight $`d`$** is proved by the tower law along a chain of prime-power levels; each
  step needs $`\mathrm{Tight}`$ at a smaller level and both halves below it (§6).

The rest of the note is the content of those two bullets.

## 3. The modular polynomial and the roots of the modular equation

Everything rests on the modular polynomial. The formalization packages the classical
$`\Phi_N`$ as a structure `ModularPolynomialData N`
([Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean, lines 215–223](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean#L215-L223)):
a bivariate $`\Phi \in \mathbb{Z}[X][Y]`$ that is

- monic in $`Y`$,
- of degree $`\psi(N)`$ in $`Y`$,
- and satisfies $`\Phi\bigl(j(q),\, j(q^N)\bigr) = 0`$.

Existence of such a monic annihilating polynomial is the integrality of $`j(q^N)`$
over $`\mathbb{Z}[j(q)]`$
([`exists_monic_evalAtJ_jqN_eq_zero`](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_exists_monic_evalAtJ_jqN_eq_zero.lean)); the degree
$`\psi(N)`$ and irreducibility are *proved from the degree computation* rather than
assumed ([`exists_phiIrreducible_of_finrank_eq`](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_exists_phiIrreducible_of_finrank_eq.lean)). For the
induction what is used at each prime $`p`$ is the explicit list of roots of
$`\Phi_p`$ over a field containing a primitive $`p`$-th root of unity. This is the
classical $`p`$-isogeny description.

**Prime-level splitting.** Let $`p`$ be prime, $`K`$ a field of characteristic $`0`$
with a primitive $`p`$-th root of unity $`\zeta_p \in K`$, and let $`u \in K^\times`$,
$`e \ge 1`$. Then

$$\Phi_p\bigl(j(u^p q^{p e}),\\, Y\bigr) \\;=\\; \bigl(Y - j(u^{p^2} q^{p^2 e})\bigr)\\ \prod_{b=0}^{p-1}\bigl(Y - j(u\\,\zeta_p^{\\,b}\\, q^{e})\bigr).$$

This is `ModularCurve.PhiGen.splits_prime_at_slot`
([Thm file, line 10](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_PhiGen_splits_prime_at_slot.lean#L10)). It is worth seeing
why it is true, because it is the only place where the modular equation is used
substantively. Classically, for the variable $`X = j(\tau)`$,

$$\Phi_p\bigl(j(\tau),\\, Y\bigr) \\;=\\; \bigl(Y - j(p\tau)\bigr)\prod_{b=0}^{p-1}\Bigl(Y - j\bigl(\tfrac{\tau+b}{p}\bigr)\Bigr):$$

the $`p+1`$ cyclic $`p`$-isogenies out of $`E_\tau`$ have quotients $`E_{p\tau}`$ and
$`E_{(\tau+b)/p}`$ (Diamond–Shurman §5.2; Lang Ch. 5). Read in the nome
$`q = e^{2\pi i \tau}`$, the formula above is just this with $`e^{2\pi i\tau} = u^p q^{pe}`$:
then $`e^{2\pi i\,p\tau} = u^{p^2}q^{p^2 e}`$ and
$`e^{2\pi i (\tau+b)/p} = u\,\zeta_p^{\,b} q^{e}`$. So the "hard" theorem is the
classical root description of $`\Phi_p`$, stated for arbitrary level data and arbitrary
$`u`$.

**Slot description of the conjugates.** Iterating the prime description and collecting
the divisors of $`M`$ gives the roots of the minimal polynomial of $`j(q^M)`$ over
$`\mathbb{Q}(j(q))`$. The Lean states it after the substitution $`q \mapsto q^M`$ of the
coefficients, which is the form in which it is consumed. For $`\zeta_M`$ a primitive
$`M`$-th root of unity,

$$\Bigl(\mathrm{minpoly}_{\mathbb{Q}(j(q))}\\, j(q^M)\Bigr)\Big|_{\\,q \mapsto q^M} \\;=\\; \prod_{a \mid M}\\ \prod_{b=0}^{M/a - 1}\\ \bigl(Y - j(\zeta_M^{ab} q^{a^2})\bigr), \qquad \gcd\bigl(\gcd(a,b),\\, M/a\bigr) = 1 .$$

This is `ModularCurve.minpoly_jqN_map_eq_prod_slots`
([Thm file, line 8](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_minpoly_jqN_map_eq_prod_slots.lean#L8)); the proof is by
induction on $`M`$ from the prime case. Two remarks on the index set.

- The pairs $`(a,b)`$ with $`a \mid M`$, $`0 \le b \lt M/a`$,
  $`\gcd(\gcd(a,b), M/a) = 1`$ number exactly $`\psi(M)`$, and they are the
  standard index set for the $`\psi(M)`$ roots of the modular equation,
  equivalently for the right cosets of $`\Gamma_0(M)`$ in
  $`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$ (whose index is $`\psi(M)`$, so the count
  matches). The exponent $`a^2`$ and the root of unity $`\zeta_M^{ab}`$ are the
  two pieces of coset data.
- The root attached to $`(a,b)`$ is $`j(\zeta_M^{ab} q^{a^2})`$; the exponent $`a^2`$
  is the denominator-square familiar from $`q`$-expansions at the cusps. For $`M=p`$
  this reads: $`j(\zeta_p^b q)`$ for $`b \lt p`$ (the $`a=1`$ slots) and $`j(q^{p^2})`$
  (the $`a=p`$ slot), matching the prime splitting after one more substitution
  $`q \mapsto q^p`$ — which is exactly the way the proof of §4 uses it.

With the level-$`M`$ nome convention $`q = e^{2\pi i\tau/M}`$, the substitution
$`q \mapsto \zeta_M^{ab}q^{a^2}`$ is the nome form of the affine self-map of the
upper half plane

$$\tau \\;\mapsto\\; a^2\tau + ab, \qquad\text{i.e. of}\qquad \begin{pmatrix} a^2 & ab \\\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathbb{Q}),$$

which is the friendly way to remember which slot a given root belongs to.

There is a second, softer computation of the same generation statement in the
*squarefree* case. At squarefree $`N`$ both relative degrees
$`[F_N : \mathbb{Q}(j)]`$ and $`[F_N^{\mathrm{full}} : \mathbb{Q}(j)]`$ can be shown to
equal $`\psi(N)`$ directly, and relative degrees multiply, so
$`[F_N^{\mathrm{full}} : F_N] = 1`$
([`functionFieldGeneration_of_squarefree`](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration_of_squarefree.lean)). That argument is
used *inside* the slot description and the non-membership lemmas below, but it is not
the general-level argument: the strong induction of §7 handles every $`N`$ by the same
descent. Where the two overlap (squarefree $`N`$) they compute the same fact, and we do
not repeat the degree-counting proof here.

## 4. Descent by one prime

This is the heart. The one-prime statement is

```lean
-- Thm_ModularCurve_jqN_div_mem_modularFunctionField.lean, line 8
theorem ModularCurve.jqN_div_mem_modularFunctionField (M : ℕ) [NeZero M] (p : ℕ) [hp : Fact (Nat.Prime p)] ...
    : jqN M ∈ modularFunctionField (M * p)
```

that is, $`j(q^M) \in \mathbb{Q}(j(q), j(q^{Mp}))`$. It says that $`j`$ at level
$`M`$ is already a rational function of $`j`$ at levels $`1`$ and $`Mp`$. The proof is
an instance of a general linear-algebra principle.

**A unique common root is rational.** Let $`F \subseteq L`$ be fields and let
$`A, B \in F[Y]`$ with $`A \ne 0`$ monic, $`A`$ splitting over $`L`$ with *distinct*
roots. If $`x \in L`$ is a common root of $`A`$ and $`B`$ and is the *unique* common
root in $`L`$, then $`x \in F`$. This is
`Polynomial.mem_range_of_unique_common_root`
([Thm file, line 5](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_Polynomial_mem_range_of_unique_common_root.lean#L5)): intuitively, the
minimal polynomial of $`x`$ over $`F`$ divides both $`A`$ and $`B`$, so it has $`x`$ as
a root; if $`x`$ were not in $`F`$ that minimal polynomial would have degree $`\gt1`$ and
some other common root.

Apply this with

$$F \\;=\\; \mathbb{Q}\bigl(j(q),\\, j(q^{Mp})\bigr), \qquad L \\;=\\; K(\\!(q)\\!), \quad K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{Mp}),$$

and the two polynomials

$$A(Y) \\;=\\; \Phi_p\bigl(j(q^{Mp}),\\, Y\bigr) \in F[Y], \qquad B(Y) \\;=\\; \text{the base change of } \mathrm{minpoly}_{\mathbb{Q}(j(q))}\bigl(j(q^M)\bigr) \text{ to } F .$$

The element $`x_0 = j(q^M)`$ is a common root: it is a root of its own minimal
polynomial $`B`$, and $`\Phi_p\bigl(j(q^{Mp}), j(q^M)\bigr) = 0`$ by the modular
equation $`\Phi_p(j(\tau), j(p\tau)) = 0`$ together with the symmetry
$`\Phi_p(X,Y) = \Phi_p(Y,X)`$ ([`EvalSymm`](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_PhiGen.lean#L139)).

It remains to see that $`x_0`$ is the *only* common root. That is where the slot
description of §3 is used, and it is a short counting argument. Over $`K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{Mp})`$,

- the roots of $`A`$ are, by the prime splitting with $`u = 1`$,
  $`e = M^2 p`$,
  $$j\bigl(q^{M^2 p^3}\bigr) \quad\text{and}\quad j\bigl(\zeta_p^{\\,b} q^{M^2 p}\bigr), \qquad b = 0,\dots,p-1 ;$$
- the roots of $`B`$ are, by the slot description,
  $$j\bigl(\zeta_M^{\\,a b'} q^{\\,p\\,a^2}\bigr), \qquad a \mid M,\\ \\ b' \lt M/a,\\ \\ \gcd\bigl(\gcd(a,b'), M/a\bigr) = 1 .$$

A common root therefore either is the "extra" root $`j(q^{M^2p^3})`$ or matches a
$`p`$-family root against a slot. Comparing the two displayed families:

- The extra root $`j(q^{M^2p^3})`$ is *not* a root of $`B`$: equality would force the
  nome exponents to agree, $`p\,a^2 = M^2p^3`$, i.e. $`a = Mp \gt M`$, contradicting
  $`a \mid M`$. This is the hypothesis called `hsp` in the Lean
  ([S file, lines 590–609](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L590-L609)).
- A $`p`$-family root has the form $`j(w\,q^{M^2p})`$ with $`w = \zeta_p^{\,b}`$.
  Matching it with the slot $`j(\zeta_M^{ab'}q^{pa^2})`$ forces
  $`pa^2 = M^2p`$, hence $`a = M`$; then the slot condition $`b' \lt M/a = 1`$ forces
  $`b' = 0`$, and the unit $`w`$ is $`1`$. Thus every common root in this family is
  $`j(q^{M^2p}) = x_0`$. The Lean packages "any common root of the shape
  $`j(w\,q^{M^2p})`$ has $`w = 1`$" as the hypothesis `htw`
  ([S file, lines 568–588](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L568-L588));
  the two hypotheses `htw` and `hsp` are *proved* from the slot description by
  `root_shape`/`htw_of`/`hsp_of`
  ([lines 541–609](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L541-L609)).

Hence $`x_0`$ is the unique common root of $`A`$ and $`B`$, and
`mem_range_of_unique_common_root` gives $`x_0 \in F`$, i.e.

$$j(q^M) \\;\in\\; \mathbb{Q}\bigl(j(q),\\, j(q^{Mp})\bigr).$$

The Lean proof then strips the coefficient extension $`\mathbb{Q} \to K`$ (coefficient
extension and $`q \mapsto q^{Mp}`$ are injective) to land back in
$`\mathbb{Q}(\!(q)\!)`$
([S file, lines 553–690](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_modularFunctionField_eq_full_of.lean#L553-L690)).

This is the precise sense in which the FLT proof is circuitous without Riemann
existence: the classical proof says "$`j`$ is a degree-$`\psi(M)`$ function and the
extra isogeny relation cuts it down"; the formal proof reconstructs the same
conclusion by listing *all* the roots and observing that exactly one can be common.

## 5. Generating the divisor field, one prime power at a time

With descent in hand, generation is a walk down the divisor lattice. Two Lean lemmas
carry it.

**Two primes beat one.** If $`p \ne q`$ are primes dividing $`d`$, and both
$`j(q^{d/p})`$ and $`j(q^{d/q})`$ lie in a field $`F`$, then $`j(q^d) \in F`$. This is
`jqN_mem_of_div_primes`
([S file, lines 397–528](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_modularFunctionField_eq_full_of.lean#L397-L528)); it is
another instance of the unique-common-root argument of §4, now run with the two
*prime-level* polynomials $`\Phi_p`$ and $`\Phi_q`$. The only input is the prime
splitting of §3. This lemma appears verbatim in more than one S file; we use it once.

**One new generator per prime power.** For $`p \nmid M`$ and $`a \ge 0`$,

$$F^{\mathrm{full}}_{M p^{a+1}} \\;=\\; \mathbb{Q}\Bigl(F^{\mathrm{full}}_{M p^{a}},\\ j(q^{p^{a+1}})\Bigr)$$

(`full_eq_adjoin_full_div_prime`
([Thm file, line 8](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_full_eq_adjoin_full_div_prime.lean#L8))). The inclusion $`\supseteq`$ is
immediate (both generators have divisor dividing $`Mp^{a+1}`$). For $`\subseteq`$, take
$`j(q^d)`$ with $`d \mid Mp^{a+1}`$; if $`d \mid Mp^a`$ it is already a generator of the
lower field. Otherwise $`d = m\,p^{a+1}`$ with $`m \mid M`$ and $`p \nmid m`$; if
$`m = 1`$ then $`j(q^d)`$ is the new generator itself, and if $`m \gt 1`$ pick a prime
$`q \mid m`$, $`q \ne p`$. Then

$$d/p = m\\,p^{a} \\ \text{ lies below the level } Mp^a, \qquad d/q \\ \text{ is a proper divisor of } d,$$

so $`j(q^{d/p})`$ is in the target by inspection and $`j(q^{d/q})`$ by strong induction
on $`d`$; the two-prime lemma gives $`j(q^{d})`$ in the target. This is
`w1_jqN_mem_adjoin_top_insert`
([S file, lines 533–592](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_full_eq_adjoin_full_div_prime.lean#L533-L592)).

**Collapse at a general level.** Now let $`d \mid N`$, $`d \ne N`$. Write
$`m = N/d \gt 1`$, let $`p`$ be a prime factor of $`m`$ and $`m = p\,m'`$, so
$`N = (d m')\,p`$. By §4 (descent), $`j(q^{dm'}) \in F_N`$, and by induction
$`\mathrm{Gen}(dm')`$, the field $`F_{dm'}`$ is the full field at level $`dm'`$, which
is contained in $`F_N`$ because every $`j(q^{d'})`$ with $`d' \mid dm'`$ is a
$`j(q^{d'})`$ with $`d' \mid N`$ and thus lies in $`F_N`$ by definition. Hence
$`j(q^d) \in F_{dm'} \subseteq F_N`$. This is exactly
`modularFunctionField_eq_full_of`
([S file, lines 694–725](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_modularFunctionField_eq_full_of.lean#L694-L725)): it reduces
$`\mathrm{Gen}(N)`$ to the two families
$`j(q^M) \in F_N`$ and $`\mathrm{Gen}(M)`$ for every factorization $`N = M\cdot p`$ with
$`p`$ prime.

That closes the generation half of the induction.

## 6. The degree of one prime-power step

The degree half mirrors the generation half at the level of prime powers. Fix
$`M`$, $`p \nmid M`$, and write $`F^{\mathrm{full}}_k := F^{\mathrm{full}}_{M p^k}`$.
The step is

$$F^{\mathrm{full}}_{a+1} \\;=\\; \mathbb{Q}\bigl(F^{\mathrm{full}}_a,\\ j(q^{p^{a+1}})\bigr) \quad\text{and}\quad j(q^{p^{a+1}}) \notin F^{\mathrm{full}}_a ,$$

the first from §5, the second from the non-membership lemmas

$$j(q^p) \notin F^{\mathrm{full}}_M \quad (p \nmid M) \qquad\text{and}\qquad j(q^{p^{a+2}}) \notin \mathbb{Q}\bigl(F^{\mathrm{full}}_M,\\ j(q^{p}),\dots,j(q^{p^{a+1}})\bigr).$$

These are `jqN_prime_not_mem_full`
([Thm file, line 8](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_jqN_prime_not_mem_full.lean#L8)) and
`jqN_pow_not_mem_adjoin_full`
([Thm file, line 8](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_jqN_pow_not_mem_adjoin_full.lean#L8)). Both again use the slot
description of §3, this time for a degree count. For the first: if $`j(q^p)`$ lay
in $`F^{\mathrm{full}}_M`$, then by $`\mathrm{Gen}(M)`$ it would lie in
$`\mathbb{Q}(j(q))(j(q^M))`$, say $`j(q^p) = g(j(q^M))`$ with
$`\deg g \lt \psi(M)`$; evaluating $`g`$ at the $`\psi(M)`$ distinct slots of the
minimal polynomial of $`j(q^M)`$ shows it takes the same value at all of them, and a
polynomial of degree $`\lt \psi(M)`$ that is constant on $`\psi(M)`$ points has its
constant value in $`\mathbb{Q}(j(q))`$ — so $`j(q^p) \in \mathbb{Q}(j(q))`$,
contradicting the prime-level degree
$`[\mathbb{Q}(j(q))(j(q^p)) : \mathbb{Q}(j(q))] = p+1`$ (which, like $`\mathrm{Gen}(p)`$,
is the squarefree case of §3). The second lemma iterates the same argument up the
$`p`$-power tower.

Given non-membership, the relative degree of the step is the degree of a minimal
polynomial, and it takes only two values:

$$\bigl[\\,F^{\mathrm{full}}_{a+1} : F^{\mathrm{full}}_a\\,\bigr] \\;=\\; \begin{cases} p+1, & a = 0,\\\\ p, & a \ge 1.\end{cases}$$

This is `relfinrank_full_eq_mul`
([S file, lines 49–72](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_relfinrank_full_eq_mul.lean#L49-L72)). It rests on two
degree statements about adjoining a $`p`$-power of $`j`$ to a field containing
$`j(q)`$:

- **(first prime power, degree $`p+1`$)** if $`j(q) \in E`$ and $`j(q^p) \notin E`$, then
  $`[E(j(q^p)) : E] = p+1`$
  ([Thm file](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_finrank_adjoin_jqN_prime_of_not_mem.lean)). This is the degree
  of $`\Phi_p`$. The minimal polynomial of $`j(q^p)`$ over $`E`$ divides
  $`\Phi_p(j(q), Y)`$; after the coefficient substitution $`q \mapsto q^p`$ and base
  change to a field containing $`\zeta_p`$ it becomes $`\Phi_p(j(q^p), Y)`$, with the
  $`p+1`$ roots $`j(q^{p^2})`$ and $`j(\zeta_p^b q)`$, $`b \lt p`$. Multiplication of
  the nome by $`\zeta_p`$ (the `qTwist` substitution, an $`E`$-algebra automorphism of
  the coefficient extension) cycles the $`p`$ roots $`j(\zeta_p^b q)`$ and fixes
  $`j(q^{p^2})`$; and $`j(q^{p^2}) \notin E`$ because $`j(q^{p^2}) \in E`$ would force
  $`j(q^p) \in E`$. The general lemma
  `Polynomial.irreducible_of_transitive_ringAut`
  ([Thm file, line 5](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_Polynomial_irreducible_of_transitive_ringAut.lean#L5)) says exactly
  this: *a monic polynomial that splits with distinct roots, one root outside the base
  field and all others cycled by a base-field automorphism, is irreducible.* Hence the
  minimal polynomial of $`j(q^p)`$ over $`E`$ has degree $`p+1`$.
- **(later prime powers, degree $`p`$)** if $`j(q^{p^k})`$ and $`j(q^{p^{k+1}})`$ lie in
  $`E`$ but $`j(q^{p^{k+2}}) \notin E`$, then
  $`[E(j(q^{p^{k+2}})) : E] = p`$
  ([Thm file](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_finrank_adjoin_jqN_pow_succ_of_not_mem.lean)). Here
  $`Q(Y) = \Phi_p(j(q^{p^{k+1}}), Y)`$ has degree $`p+1`$, but by symmetry it already
  has the root $`j(q^{p^k}) \in E`$, so it factors in $`E[Y]`$ as
  $`(Y - j(q^{p^k}))\cdot P(Y)`$ with $`\deg P = p`$; the element
  $`j(q^{p^{k+2}})`$ is a root of $`P`$ and not of the linear factor, and the same
  automorphism argument makes $`P`$ irreducible. So the minimal polynomial has degree
  $`p`$.

With the step degree known, the degree of a full prime-power step follows by the tower
law, and the Dedekind recursion

$$\psi(M p^{a+1}) \\;=\\; \begin{cases}\psi(M)(p+1), & a = 0,\\\\ \psi(M p^{a})\\,p, & a \ge 1,\end{cases}$$

which is `dedekindPsi_mul_of_coprime` and `dedekindPsi_prime_pow` packaged for this
case ([S file, lines 436–465](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L436-L465)) — matches the two
values of the step exactly. This equality of the two products is the whole content of
Tight at $`d = M p^{a+1}`$.

## 7. The induction closes

We can now say what `hall_all` does at a level $`d`$, assuming $`\mathrm{Hall}(m)`$ for
all proper divisors $`m \mid d`$.

**Step 1: `Gen d`.** If $`d = 1`$ this is `gen_one`. Otherwise use
`modularFunctionField_eq_full_of` (§5). For a factorization $`d = M\cdot p`$ with
$`p`$ prime, the descent hypothesis is the one-prime result of §4 applied at $`(M,p)`$
— its two extra hypotheses `htw`/`hsp` come from the slot description of §3 at level
$`M`$, which is available because $`\mathrm{Hall}(M)`$ holds by induction
([S file, lines 630–650](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L630-L650)). The induction
hypothesis supplies $`\mathrm{Gen}(M)`$.

**Step 2: `Tight d`.** If $`d = 1`$ this is `tight_one`. Otherwise let $`p`$ be the
smallest prime factor of $`d`$, and write $`d = M\,p^{a+1}`$ with $`p \nmid M`$
([S file, lines 653–700](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L653-L700)). Then

- $`[\,F^{\mathrm{full}}_{M p^{a}} : \mathbb{Q}(j)] = \psi(M p^{a})`$ by induction
  (`relfinrank_full_of`, [S file, lines 489–493](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L489-L493));
- the prime-power step contributes the factor $`p+1`$ or $`p`$ by §6;
- by the tower law,
  $`[\,F^{\mathrm{full}}_{d} : \mathbb{Q}(j)] = \psi(d)`$;
- and since $`\mathrm{Gen}(d)`$ was just proved, the same number is
  $`[\,\mathbb{Q}(j)(j(q^d)) : \mathbb{Q}(j)]`$ (this is the one-line identity
  `relfinrank_modularFunctionField`
  ([Thm file](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_relfinrank_modularFunctionField.lean))), which is Tight.

Strong induction concludes $`\mathrm{Hall}(N)`$ for every $`N \ne 0`$. Unwinding the
bundle at $`d = N`$ gives $`\mathrm{Gen}(N): F_N = F_N^{\mathrm{full}}`$, and
`functionFieldGeneration_iff_full_eq` converts it:

```lean
-- S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean, lines 728–729
private theorem ModularCurve.functionFieldGeneration (N : ℕ) [NeZero N] : FunctionFieldGeneration N :=
  (ModularCurve.functionFieldGeneration_iff_full_eq N).mpr (ModularCurve.modularFunctionField_eq_full N).symm
```

The same bundle yields, with no extra work, the corollaries that the rest of the
modular-curve development consumes:

- $`[\,\mathbb{Q}(j)(j(q^N)) : \mathbb{Q}(j)] = \psi(N)`$
  (`finrank_adjoin_jqN_eq_dedekindPsi`);
- an irreducible modular polynomial datum at every level
  (`exists_phiIrreducible`, via `exists_phiIrreducible_of_finrank_eq`);
- $`[\,F_N^{\mathrm{full}} : \mathbb{Q}(j)] = \psi(N)`$
  (`relfinrank_full_eq_dedekindPsi`).

Generation is what makes the degeneracy maps of [009](009-hecke-jacobian-commute.md)
land in a *single* ambient field, and the degree $`\psi(N)`$ is what lets the
Hecke-correspondence proof match ranks there; the "hard" analytic input of the whole
story is the root description of §3, and after it the two halves of the induction are
bookkeeping on the divisor lattice.

## 8. Lean detours (not mathematics)

Four formal devices recur; none of them carries mathematical content.

- **`TS K e u`, or writing a cusp value as a series.** The Lean has no moduli problem,
  no upper half plane and no Möbius action in this proof. "Substitute $`q \mapsto uq^e`$"
  is literally the composite `qExpand K e (qTwist u ...)` of the two ring
  homomorphisms, and `TS K e u` is its value on `coeffEmb K jq`
  ([S file, lines 28–29](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_modularFunctionField_eq_full_of.lean#L28-L29)). The
  injectivity of `TS` on pairs $`(e,u)`$ (from the coefficient at $`q^{-e}`$) is what
  turns "distinct roots" in §3–§4 into arithmetic on exponents.
- **Coefficient extension instead of complex analysis.** The roots of the modular
  equation live over $`\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)`$; the Lean introduces the cyclotomic field
  `CyclotomicField N ℚ`, picks a primitive root `cycUnit N`, and pushes the whole
  computation along `coeffEmb` into Laurent series over that field
  ([S file, lines 188–203](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_modularFunctionField_eq_full_of.lean#L188-L203)). The
  cyclotomic Galois action is the `qTwist` substitution; that is why
  `irreducible_of_transitive_ringAut` applies. No analytic continuation is used
  anywhere.
- **The `Hall` bundle.** `Tight`, `Gen` and `Hall` are private `abbrev`s in the S file
  ([lines 413–418](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_functionFieldGeneration.lean#L413-L418)); they exist so
  that the strong induction can carry both invariants in one hypothesis. This is why
  the file proves the two facts simultaneously rather than in two passes.
- **`NeZero` and the `if a = 0` guard.** Every level is a natural number with a
  `NeZero` instance: `qExpand R 0` would be the zero map, and `jqN 0` is not a
  $`q`$-expansion. The slot formula carries a vacuous `if a = 0` branch for the same
  reason; `a \mid M` with $`M \ne 0`$ already excludes $`a = 0`$. This is plumbing, not
  mathematics.

One genuine redundancy is worth naming. The squarefree case of generation is proved
twice in the project: once by the general strong induction here, and once directly by
degree counting (`functionFieldGeneration_of_squarefree`). The degree-counting proof is
what the slot description and the non-membership lemmas consume; the strong induction
of §7 never needs to separate the squarefree case. When reading the dependency graph,
these two are not independent mathematics.
