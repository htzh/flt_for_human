Riemann-Roch

  For a divisor D on a smooth projective curve of genus g, let L(D) be the
  space of rational functions whose poles are bounded by D, and ell(D) its
  dimension. Riemann-Roch says that L(D) and L(K - D), for K a canonical
  divisor, together account for deg D + 1 - g dimensions.

== 1. The statement ==

    ell(D) - ell(K - D) = deg D + 1 - g
    deg K = 2g - 2,   ell(K) = g,   genus = (deg K + 2) / 2

  The quantity ell(K - D) is the index of specialty: it vanishes as soon as
  deg D > 2g - 2, which gives the Riemann inequality ell(D) >= deg D + 1 - g
  with equality in that range.

  To compute ell(D) concretely, take D = n*P for a rational Weierstrass point
  P of a hyperelliptic curve y^2 = f(x), deg f = 2g + 1. The monomials x^i y^j
  have pole order 2i + (2g + 1)j at P, so those of order at most n span
  L(n*P). They are not independent: the Weierstrass relation y^2 = f(x) makes
  y^2 x^i and x^(i + 2g + 1) have the same pole order, so their difference has
  smaller order. A basis has one function per distinct order, hence

  ell(n*P) = #{ 2i + (2g+1)j <= n }, counted without multiplicity,

  i.e. the number of non-gaps at most n. For g = 0 every order is a non-gap
  and the basis is 1, x, x^2, ... .

== 2. Genus 0: the projective line ==

  On P^1 take D = n*infinity. Then L(D) is the space of polynomials of degree
  at most n, of dimension n + 1, and the canonical divisor is -2*infinity (the
  differential dt has a double pole and no zeros).

    ok   genus 0: Riemann-Roch holds for n = 0..4

== 3. Genus 1: an elliptic curve ==

  For an elliptic curve a degree-zero divisor is principal exactly when its
  points sum to O, so a canonical divisor may be taken to be 0: the
  holomorphic differential dx/(2y) has neither zeros nor poles. The non-gaps
  at O are generated by 2 and 3, so the single gap is order 1.
    concrete model: E: y^2 = x^3 + (6)*x + (0) over GF(7),  #E = 8
    n = 0:  spanning monomials 1   count 1
            basis 1   ell = 1
    n = 1:  spanning monomials 1   count 1
            basis 1   ell = 1
    n = 2:  spanning monomials 1, x   count 2
            basis 1, x   ell = 2
    n = 3:  spanning monomials 1, x, y   count 3
            basis 1, x, y   ell = 3
    n = 4:  spanning monomials 1, x, x^2, y   count 4
            basis 1, x, y, x^2   ell = 4
    n = 5:  spanning monomials 1, x, x^2, y, x*y   count 5
            basis 1, x, y, x^2, x*y   ell = 5

  At n = 6 the two counts differ: x^3 and y^2 both have pole order 6 and are
  related by y^2 = x^3 - x for this curve, so the spanning set has 7 elements
  but L(6*O) has dimension 6.

== 5. The formalization ==

  In AlgebraicCurve the ingredients of the statement are: LSpace D, the
  function space L(D); ell D = finrank K (LSpace D); and indexOfSpecialty D =
  ell (K - D). RiemannRochRows.lean defines, as Props, RiemannInequality (deg
  D + 1 - genus <= ell D), RiemannIndexFormula (indexOfSpecialty D = ell D -
  (deg D + 1 - genus)) and FunctionFieldRiemannRoch (ell D - ell (K - D) = deg
  D + 1 - genus).

  Genus is not assumed. CanonicalDivisor.lean defines it from a canonical
  divisor by genus = (deg K + 2) / 2, which is the formula checked above;
  ell(K) = g then follows from Riemann-Roch rather than being built in. In the
  FLT proof this is what converts degree counts on J_0(N) into dimension
  counts feeding the Hecke action (math/009); the genus-1 case computed here
  is the elliptic curve of base/007.
