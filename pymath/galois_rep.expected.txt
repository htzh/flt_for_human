Galois representations on E[l]

  The Galois group acts on the l-torsion of an elliptic curve, giving a
  two-dimensional representation over F_l. This demo computes that
  representation over finite fields, where the Galois group is generated by
  Frobenius and the single operator rho(Frob) is the whole representation, and
  it verifies the two structural constraints the FLT proof relies on: the
  determinant is the cyclotomic character, and the representation is odd.

== 1. The representation, over a finite field ==

  Take E: y^2 = x^3 + 1 over GF(5). Its 3-torsion is not rational over GF(5);
  it appears over GF(25), where a basis of E[3] can be chosen. The 5-power
  Frobenius then acts as an explicit 2x2 matrix with entries in Z/3, and that
  matrix *is* rho_{E,3}(Frob_5) in this basis.
    E: y^2 = x^3 + 1 over GF(5),  #E(GF(5)) = 6,  a_5 = 0
    rho(Frob) on E[3] in a basis: M = ((1, 0), (0, 2))   (entries in Z/3)
    trace(M) = 0,   det(M) = 2

== 2. The determinant is the cyclotomic character ==

  Frobenius acts on the l-th roots of unity by zeta -> zeta^p, so
  chi_l(Frob_p) = p in (Z/l)^x. The Weil pairing (base/007) makes the
  determinant of the action on E[l] the same character, so det rho(Frob) must
  be p mod l -- and it is.
    ok   det rho(Frob) = p mod l = chi_l(Frob)
    ok   trace rho(Frob) = a_p mod l
    ok   M satisfies its characteristic polynomial T^2 - a_p T + p
    constant term p is the determinant, i.e. the cyclotomic character;
    the trace a_p is the coefficient matched with a Hecke eigenvalue.

== 3. Oddness: complex conjugation acts by inversion ==

  Complex conjugation c sends zeta_n to zeta_n^(-1). That inversion is a
  Galois automorphism of Q(zeta_n) because the cyclotomic polynomial Phi_n is
  palindromic -- its roots come in inverse pairs -- and it is nontrivial for n
  > 2. Its fixed field is the real subfield, of degree phi(n)/2. So chi_n(c) =
  -1, and any representation whose determinant is chi_n is odd: det rho(c) =
  -1.
    n | Phi_n palindromic | inversion nontrivial | chi_n(c) | real degree (phi/2)
     3 | True              | True                 |       2 | 1 (1)
     4 | True              | True                 |       3 | 1 (1)
     5 | True              | True                 |       4 | 2 (2)
     6 | True              | True                 |       5 | 1 (1)
     7 | True              | True                 |       6 | 3 (3)
     8 | True              | True                 |       7 | 2 (2)
     9 | True              | True                 |       8 | 3 (3)
    10 | True              | True                 |       9 | 2 (2)
    ok   Phi_n is palindromic and inversion is a nontrivial automorphism
    ok   chi_n(c) = -1 for n > 2, hence det rho(c) = -1 (rho is odd)
    ok   the fixed field of conjugation is the real subfield of degree phi(n)/2

== 4. Frobenius and the characteristic polynomial ==

  At a good prime p the characteristic polynomial of rho(Frob_p) is T^2 - a_p
  T + p: trace a_p, determinant p. The trace is the Hecke eigenvalue side
  (hecke_eichler_shimura, modular_qexp) and the determinant is the cyclotomic
  character side (section 2).
    curve                          | a_p | trace | determinant
    y^2 = x^3 - x  over GF(7)      |   0 |     0 |           7
    y^2 = x^3 + 2  over GF(7)      |  -1 |    -1 |           7
    y^2 = x^3 + 1  over GF(5)      |   0 |     0 |           5
    y^2 = x^3 + x + 1 over GF(5)   |  -3 |    -3 |           5
    y^2 = x^3 + 15x over GF(41)    |  -8 |    -8 |          41
    ok   the Frobenius polynomial has trace a_p and constant term p mod l

== 5. Irreducible or reducible ==

  Over a finite field the representation is generated by Frobenius, and for l
  different from p it is semisimple; hence it is irreducible exactly when T^2
  - a_p T + p is irreducible over F_l. Reducibility means there is a
  Galois-stable line, i.e. an eigenvector for the matrix.
    curve                         | a_p | l=2 | l=3 | l=5 | l=7 | l=11 | l=13
    y^2 = x^3 - x  over GF(7)     |   0 | red | irr | irr | --  | red | irr
    y^2 = x^3 + 2  over GF(7)     |  -1 | irr | red | irr | --  | irr | red
    y^2 = x^3 + 1  over GF(5)     |   0 | red | red | --  | red | irr | irr
    y^2 = x^3 + x + 1 over GF(5)  |  -3 | irr | red | --  | irr | red | irr
    y^2 = x^3 + 15x over GF(41)   |  -8 | red | irr | red | irr | irr | red
    ok   the concrete matrix M is reducible, and its polynomial splits mod 3
    ok   an eigenvector of M over F_l exists exactly when the polynomial splits
    ok   E: y^2 = x^3 - x over GF(7) is irreducible at l = 3, 5, 13 and reducible at l = 2, 11

== 6. The formalization ==

  ResidualGaloisRep bundles a finite-dimensional k-vector space with a
  continuous action of the absolute Galois group of Q that factors through a
  finite level. IsOdd is the determinant condition above: det rho(c) = -1 at
  complex conjugation. IsIrreducible says there is no Galois-stable subspace,
  and IsAttachedTo records the Frobenius polynomial X^2 - a X + l linking the
  representation to a cusp form.

  For the Frey curve the proof needs GaloisRepIsIrreducible, which is
  FreyPackage.Mazur_Frey for p >= 17 (Mazur's Eisenstein-ideal argument) with
  small primes handled separately by Kummer theory and explicit descents. In
  the reducible case Serre's observation,
  FreyPackage.frey_reducible_hasCofixedLine, produces a Galois-stable line on
  which the quotient acts trivially -- the cofixed line that the rest of the
  argument rules out.
