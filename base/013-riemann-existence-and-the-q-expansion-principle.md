# Riemann existence for modular curves, and the level-one q-expansion principle

Thirteenth of the `base/` notes. [003](003-no-level-2-weight-2-cusp-forms.md)
worked the level-one vanishing statement down to the maximum-modulus principle,
[004](004-the-j-invariant.md) built the analytic and formal models of $`j`$,
[005](005-cyclic-isogenies-and-level.md) and
[006](006-the-modular-equation.md) built the modular equation and the cover
behind it, and [math/010](../math/010-function-field-generation.md) proved the
generation theorem inside $`\mathbb{Q}((q))`$. This note is about the classical
theorem none of them states: the **Riemann existence theorem**, in the form that
produces the modular curves $`X_0(N)`$ and computes the degree of the cover
$`X_0(N) \to X(1)`$.

It is written to answer one question about the FLT proof, and the answer is
stated before the machinery. The classical treatment of the modular equation uses
Riemann-surface theory in two different places:

- at **level one**, where $`X(1)`$ is compact of genus $`0`$ and $`j`$ is a
  Hauptmodul; this is what forces a holomorphic, bounded,
  $`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$-invariant $`q`$-series to be constant, and hence
  forces a pole-bounded invariant series to be a *polynomial* in $`j`$;
- at **level $`N`$**, where $`X_0(N) \to X(1)`$ is a connected cover of degree
  $`\psi(N)`$; this is what makes $`\Phi_N`$ irreducible and the function field
  generated by $`j`$ and $`j_N`$.

The formal proof keeps the first and replaces the second by algebra: it lists the
roots of the modular equation and reads the degree off the list. So "Riemann
existence specialized to $`X_0(N)`$" is really two statements of different status
in the proof, and the genuinely analytic one is the specialization to $`X(1)`$,
the base of the tower — the **level-one q-expansion principle**. §6 states that
as the answer; §§1–5 supply it.

Line-number citations point at `anthropics/fermats-last-theorem@aa2d8b3`; mathlib
citations point at tag **v4.33.0**. Both are rendered GitHub links.

## 1. The theorem, and what "existence" supplies

The Riemann existence theorem has a function-field form, and that is the form in
which modular curves use it.

**Theorem (Riemann existence).** Let $`X`$ be a compact Riemann surface and
$`M(X)`$ its field of meromorphic functions. For every finite extension
$`L/M(X)`$ there is a compact Riemann surface $`Y`$, unique up to isomorphism
over $`X`$, together with a finite morphism $`\pi : Y \to X`$ such that
$`M(Y) \cong L`$ over $`M(X)`$; the degree of $`\pi`$ equals $`[L : M(X)]`$, and
$`Y`$ is connected exactly when $`L`$ is a field. Conversely $`X \mapsto M(X)`$
is an equivalence between compact Riemann surfaces with nonconstant holomorphic
maps and finitely generated extensions of $`\mathbb{C}`$ of transcendence degree
$`1`$.

Three analytic inputs are packed into it, and it is worth separating them because
the modular-curve case uses them unequally:

1. **There are enough meromorphic functions.** On a compact Riemann surface the
   space $`L(D)`$ of functions with poles bounded by a divisor $`D`$ is finite
   dimensional, and has dimension roughly $`\deg D`$ once $`\deg D`$ exceeds
   $`2g - 2`$ (Riemann–Roch, [009](009-differentials-residues-riemann-roch.md)).
   This is what lets a function field be generated by one element and what
   computes the degree of a cover.
2. **The field determines the surface.** A compact Riemann surface is recovered
   from $`M(X)`$: meromorphic functions separate points and separate tangents, so
   $`X`$ is the normalization of the algebraic curve it defines.
3. **The quotient is compact.** Filling in the cusps turns
   $`\Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}`$ into a compact Riemann surface. Concretely, at
   a cusp the local parameter is $`q^{1/w}`$ for the cusp width $`w`$, and the
   chart $`q = 0`$ is the added point.

None of this is in mathlib, and the FLT proof never uses the theorem in this
generality. The purpose of stating it is to locate precisely which of these three
inputs survives, and in what form, in the formal proof.

**The modular-curve corollary.** Let $`\Gamma \le \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$
have finite index and let
$`\mathbb{H}^* = \mathbb{H} \cup \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})`$ be the upper
half-plane with the cusps. Then

$$Y(\Gamma) \\;=\\; \Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}, \qquad
  X(\Gamma) \\;=\\; \Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}^*$$

are Riemann surfaces, $`X(\Gamma)`$ is compact, and $`M(X(\Gamma))`$ is the field
of modular functions for $`\Gamma`$ — the $`\Gamma`$-invariant meromorphic
functions on $`\mathbb{H}`$ that are meromorphic at every cusp. For
$`\Gamma' \le \Gamma`$ of finite index the natural map

$$X(\Gamma') \\;\longrightarrow\\; X(\Gamma)$$

is a finite cover of degree $`[\Gamma : \Gamma']`$, branched only over the images
of the elliptic points and the cusps. This corollary is the classical source of
every "degree" and every "cover" statement about modular curves in these notes.

## 2. The specialization to $`X_0(N)`$

Fix $`N \ge 1`$ and

$$\Gamma_0(N) \\;=\\; \Bigl\\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\\\ c & d \end{pmatrix}
  \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \\;:\\; c \equiv 0 \pmod N \Bigr\\}.$$

Write $`X_0(N) = X(\Gamma_0(N))`$ and
$`X(1) = X(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))`$. The index is the Dedekind function

$$\psi(N) \\;=\\; [\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) : \Gamma_0(N)]
  \\;=\\; N \prod_{p \mid N} \Bigl(1 + \frac{1}{p}\Bigr),$$

the code's `dedekindPsi`
([Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean, line 201](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean#L201)),
and the same number as the count of cyclic order-$`N`$ subgroups of
$`(\mathbb{Z}/N)^2`$, equivalently of points of the projective line
$`\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Z}/N)`$
([005 §2](005-cyclic-isogenies-and-level.md)).
The Riemann-existence corollary then gives, with no further input:

- **the cover:** $`\pi_N : X_0(N) \to X(1)`$ is a finite map of degree
  $`\psi(N)`$;
- **the branch locus:** $`\pi_N`$ is branched over $`j = 0`$, $`j = 1728`$, and
  the cusp $`j = \infty`$; away from these the $`\psi(N)`$ sheets are the cyclic
  order-$`N`$ subgroups of a generic elliptic curve, so over a generic $`[E]`$
  the fibre is the set of such subgroups and is identified with
  $`\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Z}/N)`$;
- **the cusp count and genus:** the number of cusps is
  $`\nu_\infty(N) = \sum_{d \mid N} \varphi(\gcd(d, N/d))`$ and the genus is
  $`g(X_0(N)) = 1 + \psi(N)/12 - \nu_\infty(N)/2 - \nu_2(N)/4 - \nu_3(N)/3`$,
  where $`\nu_2(N)`$ and $`\nu_3(N)`$ are the numbers of elliptic points of
  order $`2`$ and $`3`$. Only $`\psi(N)`$ is used by the modular-equation
  argument; the cusp count and genus are recorded for orientation;
- **the monodromy:** $`\pi_N`$ is connected, so the monodromy action on a fibre
  is transitive. For squarefree $`N`$ this action is that of
  $`\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/N) \cong \prod_{p \mid N} \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)`$
  on
  $`\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Z}/N) = \prod_{p \mid N} \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)`$,
  which is transitive because a product of transitive actions is;
- **the function field and the equation:** the function field is
  $`M(X_0(N)) = \mathbb{C}(j, j_N)`$, with $`j_N(\tau) = j(N\tau)`$, and it is a
  degree-$`\psi(N)`$ extension of $`M(X(1)) = \mathbb{C}(j)`$. The integral model
  of the extension is the modular polynomial
  $`\Phi_N \in \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]`$, monic of degree $`\psi(N)`$ in each variable,
  symmetric, and annihilating $`(j(\tau), j(N\tau))`$
  ([006](006-the-modular-equation.md)). Because the cover is connected and
  $`j_N`$ generates the extension, the minimal polynomial of $`j_N`$ over
  $`\mathbb{C}(j)`$ has degree $`\psi(N)`$; since $`\Phi_N(j(\tau), Y)`$ is monic
  of that degree with $`j_N`$ as a root, it *is* the minimal polynomial, hence
  $`\Phi_N`$ is irreducible over $`\mathbb{C}(j)`$.

**Level one.** For $`N = 1`$ the same corollary says $`\psi(1) = 1`$, the cover
is trivial, and $`M(X(1)) = \mathbb{C}(j)`$. More usefully, $`j`$ is holomorphic
on $`\mathbb{H}`$ and has its only pole at the cusp $`\infty`$, of order exactly
$`1`$ ([004 §5](004-the-j-invariant.md)); a meromorphic function on a compact
Riemann surface has as many poles as its degree as a map to $`\mathbb{P}^1`$, so
$`j : X(1) \to \mathbb{P}^1`$ has degree $`1`$, hence is biholomorphic. Thus

$$X(1) \\;\cong\\; \mathbb{P}^1, \qquad M(X(1)) \\;=\\; \mathbb{C}(j),$$

the **Hauptmodul** property. This is the level-one half of the corollary, and it
is the half the formal proof still needs.

## 3. The two inputs, precisely: R1 and R2

It pays to isolate the two facts the modular-equation argument uses, because they
have different logical status in the formal proof. Call them R1 and R2.

### R1 — the level-one q-expansion principle

**Kernel form.** Let $`F : \mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{C}`$ and
$`c : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}`$. Suppose that for every $`\tau \in \mathbb{H}`$
the power series converges to $`F(\tau)`$,

$$F(\tau) \\;=\\; \sum_{m \ge 0} c_m\\, q(\tau)^m, \qquad q(\tau) = e^{2\pi i \tau},$$

and that $`F(\gamma\tau) = F(\tau)`$ for every
$`\gamma \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$. Then $`c_m = 0`$ for every
$`m \ne 0`$. In words: **a holomorphic
$`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$-invariant $`q`$-series is constant.** The hypothesis
$`m \ge 0`$ is exactly holomorphy at the cusp; the invariance is weight zero; the
conclusion is the vanishing of the nonconstant Fourier coefficients.

**Hauptmodul form.** Let $`f \in \mathbb{C}((q))`$ be realized by such an
invariant $`F`$ and have pole order at most $`n`$ at $`\infty`$ (no $`q^m`$ with
$`m \lt -n`$). Then $`f \in \mathbb{C}[j]`$, with $`\deg f \le n`$. Equivalently,
the level-one modular functions that are holomorphic on $`\mathbb{H}`$ and have a
pole of order at most $`n`$ at the cusp are exactly the polynomials in $`j`$ of
degree at most $`n`$.

The two forms are the same theorem: the Hauptmodul form is the kernel applied to
$`f - P(j)`$, where $`P`$ is chosen so that the difference is holomorphic at
$`\infty`$.

**Where the analysis is.** $`F`$ descends to a holomorphic function $`G`$ on the
punctured disc $`0 \lt |q| \lt 1`$ because it is $`1`$-periodic in $`\tau`$; the
power-series hypothesis extends $`G`$ holomorphically across $`q = 0`$. So $`F`$
is a holomorphic function on the compact Riemann surface $`X(1)`$ (compact
because the cusp is filled in, connected because $`\mathbb{H}`$ is). Liouville's
theorem on a compact connected Riemann surface — if $`|F|`$ attains a maximum,
$`F`$ is constant — gives R1. The fundamental-domain version of the same
argument is carried out in
[003 §4.2](003-no-level-2-weight-2-cusp-forms.md): move any $`\tau`$ to
$`\mathrm{Im}\,\tau \ge 1/2`$, dominate $`|F(\tau)|`$ by $`|F(\xi)|`$ with
$`|q(\xi)| \le e^{-\pi}`$, and apply the maximum modulus principle.

**A term warning.** This is *not* Katz's q-expansion principle, which says that a
modular form over a base ring is determined by its $`q`$-expansion (injectivity
of the $`q`$-expansion map). R1 is stronger and different in kind: it computes the
space of holomorphic weight-zero forms as the constants. Both rest on $`X(1)`$
having no nonconstant holomorphic functions, but Katz's statement is a
determination statement, whereas R1 is a vanishing statement.

### R2 — degree and connectedness at level $`N`$

The facts the modular-equation argument needs at general level are:

- $`[M(X_0(N)) : M(X(1))] = \psi(N)`$;
- the cover $`X_0(N) \to X(1)`$ is connected, equivalently its monodromy on the
  $`\psi(N)`$ sheets is transitive, equivalently $`\Phi_N`$ is irreducible over
  $`\mathbb{C}(j)`$;
- $`M(X_0(N)) = \mathbb{C}(j, j_N)`$.

Classically all three are read off the cover of §2: finiteness from the finite
index, the degree from the number of sheets, connectedness from the connectedness
of $`\mathbb{H}`$ (or, equivalently, from transitivity of the monodromy), and
generation from the fact that the meromorphic function $`j_N`$ on the cover
separates the sheets. This is the second place the classical argument spends
Riemann-surface theory.

## 4. What the formal proof does instead

The formal proof has no compact Riemann surface and no monodromy. It works in a
single fixed field of Laurent series, $`\mathbb{Q}((q))`$ and its coefficient
extensions, and it replaces R2 by an explicit root list. Two mechanisms carry the
replacement, and both are pure algebra.

### 4.1 The prime splitting as a root list

The classical root description of $`\Phi_p`$ — the $`p + 1`$ quotients of
$`E_\tau`$ by its cyclic order-$`p`$ subgroups — is stated as a polynomial
identity in $`K((q))`$ for any field $`K`$ of characteristic $`0`$ containing
$`\zeta_p`$:

$$\Phi_p\bigl(j(u^p q^{pe}),\\, Y\bigr) \\;=\\;
  \bigl(Y - j(u^{p^2} q^{p^2 e})\bigr)
  \prod_{b=0}^{p-1}\bigl(Y - j(u\\,\zeta_p^{\\,b}\\,q^{e})\bigr).$$

This is `ModularCurve.PhiGen.splits_prime_at_slot`
([Thm file, line 10](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_PhiGen_splits_prime_at_slot.lean#L10));
[006 §3](006-the-modular-equation.md) reads its geometry. The point here is that
it turns the cover into a *list*: the $`\psi(p) = p + 1`$ roots are written down,
and no topology is needed to know what they are. Iterating over the divisor
lattice gives the full list of $`\psi(M)`$ conjugates of $`j(q^M)`$
(`minpoly_jqN_map_eq_prod_slots`).

### 4.2 Unique common root, in place of monodromy

With the root list in hand, the degree and generation statements of R2 become an
argument about common roots. If $`A`$ is monic and splits with distinct roots
over a field $`L`$, $`x`$ is a common root of $`A`$ and $`B`$, and $`x`$ is the
*unique* common root, then $`x`$ lies in the base field
(`Polynomial.mem_range_of_unique_common_root`
([Thm file, line 5](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_Polynomial_mem_range_of_unique_common_root.lean#L5))).
Applied to $`\Phi_p`$ and the minimal polynomial of $`j(q^M)`$ it gives the
descent $`j(q^M) \in \mathbb{Q}(j, j(q^{Mp}))`$; iterated over the divisor
lattice it gives generation, and with the non-membership lemmas it gives the
degree $`\psi(N)`$. This is the route of
[math/010 §§4–7](../math/010-function-field-generation.md); the role played there
by "the cover has $`\psi(N)`$ sheets and is connected" is played here by "there
are $`\psi(N)`$ slots and exactly one of them can be common".

**Monodromy as a field automorphism.** The other half of R2 — connectedness, and
hence irreducibility of $`\Phi_p`$ — also becomes algebraic. Multiplication of
the nome, $`q \mapsto \zeta_p q`$ (the code's `qTwist`
([Def_ModularCurve_PhiGen.lean, line 35](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_PhiGen.lean#L35))),
fixes $`j(q^p)`$ and $`j(q^{p^2})`$ and cycles the $`p`$ finite-slope roots
$`j(\zeta_p^b q)`$. It is therefore an automorphism of the base field
$`\mathbb{Q}(j(q^p))`$ that cycles all roots but one, and

`Polynomial.irreducible_of_transitive_ringAut`
([Thm file, line 5](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_Polynomial_irreducible_of_transitive_ringAut.lean#L5))

turns that into irreducibility. This is exactly the local monodromy at the cusp
([006 §3](006-the-modular-equation.md) calls it the **twist**), so R2's
"transitive monodromy" is replaced by "a base-field automorphism cycles the
roots".

### 4.3 The one analytic input that remains: R1, at level one

R2, then, is reconstructed algebraically, and this is the essential point: the
formal proof does *not* need the compactness or connectedness of $`X_0(N)`$.
What it still needs is R1, and it needs R1 only at level one. The reason is the
coset construction of [006 §6](006-the-modular-equation.md): the descended
coefficients $`c_k`$ of the modular polynomial are not merely
$`\Gamma_0(\ell)`$-invariant, they are realized by
$`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$-invariant functions. The code states the invariance
of the Hecke-coset polynomial

$$\bigl(X - F(w_\ell \tau)\bigr)\prod_{b=0}^{\ell-1}\bigl(X - F((\tau+b)/\ell)\bigr)$$

as `ModularCurve.cosetPoly_smul`
([Thm file, line 5](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_cosetPoly_smul.lean#L5)),
and realizes its coefficients by $`F = jt = E_4^3/\Delta`$ in
`PhiGenDescends.hasSum_cosetPoly_coeff`
([S file, line 277](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_PhiGen_PhiGenDescends_hasSum_cosetPoly_coeff.lean#L277)).
So the constancy lemma is applied with
$`\Gamma = \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$, and the level-$`N`$ q-expansion
principle — which would need the same constancy for $`\Gamma_0(N)`$, i.e. the
compactness *and* connectedness of $`X_0(N)`$ — is never invoked.

## 5. The FLT segment, mathematically

This section follows the analytic part of the modular-equation argument from the
bottom up, giving the mathematical meaning of each declaration. The chain is:

`hasSum_qParam_*` → `RealL` and its closure → `exists_aeval_jq_sub_holomorphicAtInfty`
→ `coeff_eq_zero_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant` → `mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant`
→ `PhiGen.mem_adjoin_jq_of_phiGenDescends`.

### 5.1 Realization on $`\mathbb{H}`$: `HasSum` and `RealL`

The formal proof never says "$`q`$-expansion" abstractly. It says: a Laurent
series $`A \in \mathbb{C}((q))`$ is *realized* by a function
$`F : \mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{C}`$ if for every $`\tau`$ the series converges to
$`F(\tau)`$:

$$\mathrm{RealL}(h, A, F) \\;:\Longleftrightarrow\\;
  \forall\\, \tau \in \mathbb{H}, \quad
  \sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} A_m\\, q(\tau)^m \\;=\\; F(\tau),
  \qquad q(\tau) = e^{2\pi i \tau / h}.$$

This is `ModularCurve.RealL`
([S file, line 30](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant.lean#L30)).
The general period $`h`$ is not decoration: the Hecke translates of §5.2 change
the period, and $`h = 1`$ is used only for the final constancy step. Since
$`|q(\tau)| = e^{-2\pi \mathrm{Im}(\tau)/h} \lt 1`$, every such series converges
absolutely, which is why the closure lemmas below are legitimate.

That the analytic $`j = E_4^3/\Delta`$ and the formal `jq` agree is
`ModularCurve.hasSum_jq_qParam`
([Thm file, line 7](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_hasSum_jq_qParam.lean#L7)):

$$\forall\\, \tau, \quad \sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} \bigl(\mathrm{jq.coeff}\\; m\bigr)\\, q(\tau)^m
  \\;=\\; j(\tau) \\;=\\; \frac{E_4(\tau)^3}{\Delta(\tau)},$$

together with the $`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$-invariance of the right-hand side
`E4_cube_div_discriminant_smul`
([Thm file, line 6](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_E4_cube_div_discriminant_smul.lean#L6)).
The formal `jq` is the algebraic object defined by its $`q`$-expansion
([Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean, line 157](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_X0.lean#L157));
`hasSum_jq_qParam` is the bridge that lets the analytic step apply to it.

### 5.2 Closure, and the Hecke translates

`RealL` is closed under $`+`$, $`-`$, $`\cdot`$, and finite products, and the
product case is the Cauchy product on the annulus $`0 \lt |q| \lt 1`$: if $`A`$
is realized by $`F`$ and $`B`$ by $`G`$, then $`AB`$ is realized by $`FG`$. The
Laurent case is `ModularCurve.hasSum_qParam_mul_laurent`
([Thm file, line 6](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_hasSum_qParam_mul_laurent.lean#L6));
the power-series case is `hasSum_qParam_mul`. From this, `RealL` inherits
polynomial closure: for $`P \in \mathbb{Q}[X]`$, the series $`P(jq)`$ is
realized by $`P(j(\tau))`$ (`realL_aeval_jq`).

Two Hecke translations change the period and the coefficients:

- $`\tau \mapsto (\tau + b)/\ell`$ (`heckeMatrix ℓ b`) acts on the
  $`q`$-expansion by the coefficient twist
  $`A_m \mapsto e^{2\pi i b m/\ell} A_m`$ and the period $`1 \mapsto \ell`$
  (`ModularCurve.hasSum_qParam_heckeMatrix_smul`
  ([Thm file, line 8](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_hasSum_qParam_heckeMatrix_smul.lean#L8)));
- $`\tau \mapsto \ell\tau`$ (`heckeDiagMatrix ℓ`) acts by the substitution
  $`q \mapsto q^{\ell^2}`$, i.e. by `qExpand ℂ (ℓ*ℓ)`
  (`ModularCurve.hasSum_qParam_heckeDiagMatrix_smul`
  ([Thm file, line 8](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Theorems/Thm_ModularCurve_hasSum_qParam_heckeDiagMatrix_smul.lean#L8))).

These are the analytic face of the Hecke action: the two substitutions generate
the $`\ell+1`$ cosets of the Hecke correspondence, and they are also the reason
the `RealL` predicate is stated for general $`h`$. Nothing compact is used here;
it is the algebra of the disc $`0 \lt |q| \lt 1`$.

### 5.3 Pole killing: `exists_aeval_jq_sub_holomorphicAtInfty`

Write `PoleOrderLE f n` for "$`f`$ has no $`q^m`$ with $`m \lt -n`$"
([Def_ModularCurve_PhiGen.lean, line 151](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/Definitions/Def_ModularCurve_PhiGen.lean#L151)).
Then

```lean
-- Thm_ModularCurve_exists_aeval_jq_sub_holomorphicAtInfty.lean, line 7
theorem ModularCurve.exists_aeval_jq_sub_holomorphicAtInfty (n : ℕ) :
    ∀ f : LaurentSeries ℚ, PoleOrderLE f n →
      ∃ P : Polynomial ℚ, P.natDegree ≤ n ∧ PoleOrderLE (f - Polynomial.aeval jq P) 0
```

This is pure Laurent algebra, and its content is the triangularity of the powers
of $`j`$. Because $`j = q^{-1}(1 + 744q + \cdots)`$, the coefficient of
$`q^{-k}`$ in $`j^k`$ is $`1`$ and in $`j^i`$ with $`i \lt k`$ is $`0`$; so
subtracting a suitable multiple of $`j^n`$ kills the $`q^{-n}`$ coefficient
without disturbing any higher exponent, and descending induction on $`n`$ kills
the whole principal part. Mathematically: every principal part of pole order at
most $`n`$ has a unique representative in $`\mathbb{Q}[j]`$ of degree at most
$`n`$, i.e. the map

$$P \\;\longmapsto\\; P(j) \bmod \mathbb{Q}[\\![q]\\!]$$

is an isomorphism from the polynomials of degree at most $`n`$ onto the class of
$`q^{-n}\mathbb{Q}[q^{-1}]`$ modulo nonnegative powers. This is the quantitative
form of the **Hauptmodul** property: $`j`$, the function with a single simple
pole, spans every allowed principal part.

### 5.4 The kernel: `coeff_eq_zero_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant`

```lean
-- Thm_ModularCurve_coeff_eq_zero_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant.lean, line 6
theorem ModularCurve.coeff_eq_zero_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant
    {F : UpperHalfPlane → ℂ} {c : ℕ → ℂ}
    (hF : ∀ τ, HasSum (fun m : ℕ => c m * qParam 1 (τ : ℂ) ^ m) (F τ))
    (hinv : ∀ (γ : SL(2, ℤ)) (τ : UpperHalfPlane), F (γ • τ) = F τ)
    {m : ℕ} (hm : m ≠ 0) : c m = 0
```

This is R1 in kernel form: **a holomorphic
$`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$-invariant $`q`$-series is constant**. The proof has
two halves, and the boundary between them is the whole point of the note.

*Algebraic half (the code's own work).* The hypothesis `hF` with exponents
$`m \ge 0`$ turns the series into a power series $`G(q) = \sum c_m q^m`$ on the
punctured disc, and `hasSum_cuspFunction_punctured` /
`hasFPowerSeriesOnBall_update` show $`G`$ extends holomorphically over $`q = 0`$;
`cuspFunction` is mathlib's name for this descended function
([QExpansion.lean, line 77](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/v4.33.0/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/QExpansion.lean#L77)).
From the same data the code gets that $`F`$ is complex-differentiable
(`Realized.mdifferentiable`), bounded at the cusp
(`Realized.isBoundedAtImInfty`), and periodic; and it packages $`F`$ as a genuine
`ModularForm 𝒮ℒ 0`, with the slash-invariance supplied by `hinv` and the
boundedness at cusps by `isBoundedAtImInfty`
([S file, lines 160–170](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_coeff_eq_zero_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant.lean#L160-L170)).

*Analytic half (mathlib's).* `ModularForm.eq_const_of_weight_zero`
([NormTrace.lean, line 164](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/v4.33.0/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/NormTrace.lean#L164))
says every weight-zero modular form is constant. Its proof reduces a general
arithmetic level to level one by taking the norm, and the level-one input is the
(private) lemma `levelOne_nonpos_wt_const`
([LevelOne/Basic.lean, line 89](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/v4.33.0/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/Basic.lean#L89)),
which is the fundamental-domain + maximum-modulus argument worked out in
[003 §4.2](003-no-level-2-weight-2-cusp-forms.md); its public form is
`ModularFormClass.levelOne_weight_zero_const`
([LevelOne/Basic.lean, line 103](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/v4.33.0/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/Basic.lean#L103)).
The analytic core is `cuspFunction_eqOn_const_of_nonpos_wt`
([LevelOne/Basic.lean, line 74](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/v4.33.0/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/Basic.lean#L74)),
which is Liouville on $`X(1)`$: every value of $`|G|`$ on the disc is dominated
by a value on a compact subdisc, so the maximum modulus principle forces $`G`$
constant. Finally the constant form has $`q`$-expansion $`c_0`$, so $`c_m = 0`$
for $`m \ne 0`$.

Thus R1's analysis is confined to one mathlib theorem, and the code around it is
bookkeeping: turning `HasSum` on $`\mathbb{H}`$ into a power series on the disc
and back.

### 5.5 The headline: `mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant`

```lean
-- Thm_ModularCurve_mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant.lean, line 9
theorem ModularCurve.mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant
    (f : LaurentSeries ℚ) (F : UpperHalfPlane → ℂ)
    (hF : ∀ τ, HasSum (fun m : ℤ => ((f.coeff m : ℚ) : ℂ) * qParam 1 (τ : ℂ) ^ m) (F τ))
    (hinv : ∀ (γ : SL(2, ℤ)) (τ : UpperHalfPlane), F (γ • τ) = F τ) :
    f ∈ Algebra.adjoin ℚ {jq}
```

This is R1 in Hauptmodul form. Its proof is exactly §5.3 followed by §5.4.
Choose $`n`$ with `PoleOrderLE f n` (always possible); kill the pole with $`P`$
of degree $`\le n`$; the difference $`e = f - P(jq)`$ is a power series realized
by the invariant function $`F - P(j(\tau))`$, so every positive coefficient of
$`e`$ vanishes and $`e`$ is its constant term; hence
$`f = e_0 + P(jq) \in \mathbb{Q}[jq]`$. The two ingredients are the Laurent
triangularity of §5.3 and the constancy lemma of §5.4; the result is the math
statement

> a level-one modular function that is holomorphic on $`\mathbb{H}`$ and has a
> pole of order at most $`n`$ at the cusp is a polynomial in $`j`$ of degree at
> most $`n`$.

The internal version with the realization predicate made explicit is
`ModularCurve.mem_adjoin_jq_of_realL_invariant`
([S file, line 155](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem/blob/aa2d8b3/P2M/Sol/S_ModularCurve_mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant.lean#L155)),
where `RealL h A F` is §5.1's predicate; the exported theorem above is the same
statement with the predicate unfolded into a `HasSum` hypothesis. The
modular-curve development consumes the exported form; §5.6 is one consumer.

## 6. Which specialization is the analytic input?

Here is the answer the note exists to give.

**R1 is the Riemann-existence input, and it is specialized to $`X(1)`$, not to
$`X_0(N)`$.** R1 *is* the statement that $`X(1)`$ is compact and connected, so
that Liouville's theorem applies: the only holomorphic functions on $`X(1)`$ are
the constants. Equivalently, the $`q`$-expansion is a coordinate at the cusp and
there are no nonconstant holomorphic weight-zero forms. Its classical proof is
the valence/fundamental-domain computation of the field
$`M(X(1)) = \mathbb{C}(j)`$; the formal proof receives it as
`ModularForm.eq_const_of_weight_zero` and keeps it as the one genuinely analytic
step of the modular-equation argument.

**R2 is also a Riemann-existence statement, but it is not an analytic input of
the formal proof.** R2 — degree $`\psi(N)`$, connectedness of the cover,
generation of the function field — is what general Riemann-surface theory gives
classically. The formal proof replaces it by the explicit root list of
$`\Phi_p`$, the unique-common-root lemma, and the twist as a base-field
automorphism. That is a complete substitute: the degree $`\psi(N)`$, the
irreducibility of $`\Phi_N`$, and $`M(X_0(N)) = \mathbb{C}(j, j_N)`$ are all
obtained without ever compactifying $`\Gamma_0(N) \backslash \mathbb{H}`$. So R2
is *not* what must be isolated as "the analysis the proof cannot avoid".

**Consequences for isolating the input.**

- The right general level of the analytic statement is $`X(1)`$, the base of the
  tower. This is why the invariant in the constancy lemma is
  $`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})`$ and not $`\Gamma_0(N)`$: the formal proof
  arranges the descended coefficients to be level-one invariant through the
  Hecke-coset construction (§4.3), which is what lets it avoid the level-$`N`$
  q-expansion principle and hence the compactness of $`X_0(N)`$.
- The kernel is the minimal statement. The full headline
  `mem_adjoin_jq_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant` is the kernel plus the Laurent
  triangularity of §5.3; the triangularity is algebra, not analysis, and the
  consumer needs the headline only because it wants a statement about
  $`\mathbb{Q}[jq]`$. If one wants to name *the* Riemann-existence input, name
  the kernel `coeff_eq_zero_of_hasSum_of_slash_invariant`; state the headline on
  top.
- The realization hypothesis is essential and is where the analysis hides. The
  formal statement without it is false: $`f = 1 + q`$ is a nonconstant power
  series in $`\mathbb{Q}((q))`$, but it is not a polynomial in `jq`, and the
  reason is precisely that Liouville on $`X(1)`$ forbids a nonconstant
  holomorphic invariant function realizing it. Any reformulation of the input
  that drops the `HasSum` on $`\mathbb{H}`$ drops the mathematics with it.

**On the two candidates.** If one describes the input as "$`j`$ is a
degree-$`\psi(M)`$ function on $`X_0(M)`$", that is R2, and the formal proof shows
R2 has an algebraic substitute. If one describes it as "a holomorphic invariant
$`q`$-series is constant", that is R1, and no algebraic substitute is known: it
is the one statement in the modular-equation argument that genuinely needs the
compactness and connectedness of a modular curve. The recommendation to isolate
R1, and the warning that R1 and R2 are different statements, are both correct;
the analytic gap the formal proof leaves open is R1, at level one.
